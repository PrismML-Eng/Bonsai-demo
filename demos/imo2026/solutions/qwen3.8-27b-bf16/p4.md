\[
\boxed{\text{Mulan can guarantee a win exactly for } \theta=\frac{180^\circ}{n}\text{ with }n\in\mathbb Z,\ n\ge 2.}
\]

We give a complete proof.

Throughout, angles are measured in degrees. Call an angle a **\(\theta\)-multiple** if it is of the form \(k\theta\) for some integer \(k\ge 1\).

A basic fact about the cut will be used repeatedly. Suppose a triangle \(ABC\) has angles

\[
A,\quad B,\quad C.
\]

Let \(Q\) be a point on side \(AB\), different from \(A\) and \(B\), and let

\[
x=\angle ACQ.
\]

Then the two triangles produced by cutting \(CQ\) have angles

\[
(A,\ x,\ 180^\circ-A-x)
\]

and

\[
(B,\ C-x,\ A+x).
\]

Conversely, for every real number \(x\) with \(0<x<C\), there is exactly one point \(Q\in AB\) such that \(\angle ACQ=x\): draw the ray from \(C\) inside \(\angle ACB\) making angle \(x\) with \(CA\); it meets \(AB\) at the desired point.

---

## 1. A useful lemma: once a \(\theta\)-multiple appears, Mulan can force a win

**Lemma.** If a triangle has an angle equal to \(k\theta\) for some integer \(k\ge 1\), then Mulan can force victory in at most \(k-1\) further cuts.

**Proof.** The case \(k=1\) is immediate, since the triangle already has an angle \(\theta\).

Assume \(k\ge 2\), and suppose the angle \(C=k\theta\). Mulan cuts from a point \(Q\) on the side opposite \(C\) to \(C\), choosing \(Q\) so that the angle at \(C\) is split into

\[
\theta \quad\text{and}\quad (k-1)\theta.
\]

This is possible because \(0<\theta<k\theta\). One of the two resulting triangles has an angle \(\theta\), so it is already winning for Mulan. The other has an angle \((k-1)\theta\). By induction on \(k\), from that other triangle Mulan can force victory in at most \((k-1)-1=k-2\) further cuts. Thus the total is at most

\[
1+(k-2)=k-1.
\]

∎

---

## 2. The winning values: \(\theta=180^\circ/n\)

Assume

\[
180^\circ=n\theta
\]

for some integer \(n\ge 2\). We show that from every initial triangle, Mulan can force a win in finitely many moves.

There are two cases for an arbitrary triangle \(T\).

### Case A: \(T\) already contains a \(\theta\)-multiple

Then the lemma applies immediately.

### Case B: \(T\) contains no \(\theta\)-multiple

We show that Mulan can make one cut after which **both** resulting triangles contain a \(\theta\)-multiple. Then, by the lemma, whichever triangle Shan-Yu keeps, Mulan can still force a win.

---

### Subcase \(n=2\), i.e. \(\theta=90^\circ\)

If \(T\) has no \(\theta\)-multiple, then it has no right angle. Every non-right triangle has at least two acute angles. Let those two acute angles be \(A\) and \(B\), and let \(C\) be the remaining angle.

Cut from a point \(Q\) on side \(AB\) to \(C\), choosing

\[
x=90^\circ-A.
\]

Since \(A<90^\circ\), we have \(x>0\). Also \(B<90^\circ\), and

\[
C-x=180^\circ-A-B-(90^\circ-A)=90^\circ-B>0.
\]

Thus \(0<x<C\), so the point \(Q\) exists.

The two new triangles have angles at \(Q\)

\[
180^\circ-A-x=90^\circ
\]

and

\[
A+x=90^\circ.
\]

So both resulting triangles contain a right angle, i.e. an angle \(\theta\). Mulan wins immediately.

---

### Subcase \(n\ge 3\)

Since \(T\) contains no \(\theta\)-multiple, in particular it contains no angle \(\theta\). The three angles sum to

\[
n\theta.
\]

They cannot all be \(<\theta\), because then their sum would be \(<n\theta\). Therefore at least one angle is \(>\theta\). Call that angle \(C\), and call the other two angles \(A\) and \(B\). Thus

\[
A+B+C=n\theta,\qquad C>\theta.
\]

We want to choose an integer \(m\) such that

\[
\frac{B}{\theta}<m<n-\frac{A}{\theta}.
\]

The length of this open interval is

\[
\left(n-\frac{A}{\theta}\right)-\frac{B}{\theta}
=\frac{n\theta-A-B}{\theta}
=\frac{C}{\theta}>1.
\]

Any open interval of length \(>1\) contains an integer. For example, we may take

\[
m=\left\lfloor \frac{B}{\theta}\right\rfloor+1.
\]

Then

\[
\frac{B}{\theta}<m\le \frac{B}{\theta}+1<\frac{B}{\theta}+\frac{C}{\theta}
=\frac{B+C}{\theta}
=\frac{n\theta-A}{\theta}
=n-\frac{A}{\theta}.
\]

Thus

\[
B<m\theta
\]

and

\[
A<(n-m)\theta.
\]

Also \(1\le m\le n-1\).

Now choose \(Q\in AB\) such that

\[
x=(n-m)\theta-A.
\]

We check that \(0<x<C\). Since \(A<(n-m)\theta\), we have \(x>0\). Also

\[
C-x
= n\theta-A-B-\bigl((n-m)\theta-A\bigr)
= m\theta-B>0,
\]

because \(B<m\theta\). Hence \(0<x<C\), so the point \(Q\) exists.

For the two resulting triangles, the angles at \(Q\) are

\[
180^\circ-A-x
= n\theta-A-\bigl((n-m)\theta-A\bigr)
= m\theta
\]

and

\[
A+x
= A+\bigl((n-m)\theta-A\bigr)
=(n-m)\theta.
\]

Both are positive integer multiples of \(\theta\). Therefore both resulting triangles contain a \(\theta\)-multiple.

By the lemma, Mulan can force a win from either one.

---

Thus, when \(180^\circ/\theta\) is an integer, Mulan has a winning strategy. In fact, the number of cuts needed is uniformly bounded: after the first cut in Case B, the largest possible \(\theta\)-multiple is \((n-1)\theta\), so at most \(n-2\) additional cuts are needed. Hence the total number of cuts is at most \(n-1\).

---

## 3. The non-winning values: \(180^\circ/\theta\) not an integer

Now suppose

\[
\frac{180^\circ}{\theta}\notin\mathbb Z.
\]

We show that Shan-Yu can prevent Mulan from ever winning.

Call a triangle **clean** if none of its angles is a positive integer multiple of \(\theta\). Shan-Yu will maintain cleanliness forever.

### Choosing an initial clean triangle

Only finitely many positive \(\theta\)-multiples can occur as angles of a triangle, namely

\[
\theta,2\theta,\ldots,N\theta,
\]

where

\[
N=\left\lfloor \frac{180^\circ}{\theta}\right\rfloor.
\]

Therefore it is possible to choose a triangle whose three angles avoid all of these values. For instance, take

\[
A=\frac{\theta}{2}.
\]

This is not a \(\theta\)-multiple. Let

\[
R=180^\circ-A.
\]

Choose \(B\in(0,R)\) so that neither \(B\) nor \(R-B\) is a \(\theta\)-multiple; this is possible because there are only finitely many forbidden values for \(B\). Set

\[
C=R-B.
\]

Then \(A,B,C>0\), \(A+B+C=180^\circ\), and none of them is a \(\theta\)-multiple. Shan-Yu begins with this clean triangle.

---

### Maintaining cleanliness

Suppose the current clean triangle is \(ABC\), with angles \(A,B,C\). Suppose Mulan cuts from a point \(Q\in AB\) to the opposite vertex \(C\), and let

\[
x=\angle ACQ.
\]

The two resulting triangles have angles

\[
(A,\ x,\ 180^\circ-A-x)
\]

and

\[
(B,\ C-x,\ A+x).
\]

We prove that at least one of these two triangles is clean.

Assume for contradiction that both are not clean. Since \(A\) and \(B\) are not \(\theta\)-multiples, the first triangle’s \(\theta\)-multiple angle must be either

\[
x \quad\text{or}\quad 180^\circ-A-x,
\]

and the second triangle’s \(\theta\)-multiple angle must be either

\[
C-x \quad\text{or}\quad A+x.
\]

There are four possibilities.

1. \(x\) and \(C-x\) are both \(\theta\)-multiples.  
   Then their sum \(C\) is a \(\theta\)-multiple, contradiction.

2. \(x\) and \(A+x\) are both \(\theta\)-multiples.  
   Then their difference \(A\) is a \(\theta\)-multiple, contradiction.

3. \(180^\circ-A-x\) and \(C-x\) are both \(\theta\)-multiples.  
   Their difference is
   \[
   (180^\circ-A-x)-(C-x)=180^\circ-A-C=B,
   \]
   so \(B\) is a \(\theta\)-multiple, contradiction.

4. \(180^\circ-A-x\) and \(A+x\) are both \(\theta\)-multiples.  
   Their sum is
   \[
   (180^\circ-A-x)+(A+x)=180^\circ,
   \]
   so \(180^\circ\) would be a \(\theta\)-multiple, contradicting the assumption
   \[
   \frac{180^\circ}{\theta}\notin\mathbb Z.
   \]

All four possibilities are impossible. Therefore at least one of the two resulting triangles is clean. Shan-Yu keeps a clean triangle.

By induction, after every move the triangle remains clean. In particular, it never contains an angle \(\theta\), so the game never stops. Thus Mulan cannot guarantee victory.

---

## Conclusion

Mulan can guarantee victory in finitely many steps exactly when

\[
\frac{180^\circ}{\theta}\in\mathbb Z.
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{\theta=\frac{180^\circ}{n}\quad\text{for some integer }n\ge 2.}
\]
