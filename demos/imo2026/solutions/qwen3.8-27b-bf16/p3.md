We will prove that

\[
\boxed{c_n=\frac{2^n}{2^{n+1}-1}}
\]

for every positive integer \(n\).

We shall use the following standard fact about the final picking game.

**Lemma 1.** Let the final pieces have lengths
\[
x_1\ge x_2\ge\cdots\ge x_m,\qquad \sum x_i=1.
\]
Under optimal play, the first player obtains

\[
F(x_1,\dots,x_m)=x_1+x_3+x_5+\cdots.
\]

Equivalently, the second player can guarantee

\[
S(x_1,\dots,x_m)=x_2+x_4+\cdots=1-F.
\]

*Proof.* For \(m=1,2\) this is clear. For the general case, the first player takes the largest piece \(x_1\). On the remaining multiset the second player is first, and by induction obtains
\[
x_2+x_4+\cdots.
\]
Hence the first player obtains \(x_1+x_3+x_5+\cdots\).

Conversely, the second player can guarantee \(x_2+x_4+\cdots\) by the following pairing strategy. If \(m\) is even, pair
\[
(x_1,x_2),(x_3,x_4),\dots.
\]
If \(m\) is odd, leave \(x_m\) unpaired and pair
\[
(x_1,x_2),(x_3,x_4),\dots,(x_{m-3},x_{m-2}).
\]
Whenever the first player takes a piece from a pair, the second takes the other piece in that pair. In each pair the second player's piece is at least as large as the first player's piece from that pair, and if there is an unpaired piece it is the smallest one. Thus the second player obtains at least
\[
x_2+x_4+\cdots.
\]
∎

Therefore, after all cuts have been made, Liu's total equals the sum of the odd-indexed final lengths, and Xiang's total equals the sum of the even-indexed final lengths, in decreasing order.

## 1. Liu can guarantee \(\displaystyle \frac{2^n}{2^{n+1}-1}\)

Liu marks \(n\) points so that the stick is divided into \(n+1\) pieces of lengths

\[
\frac1T,\frac2T,\frac4T,\dots,\frac{2^n}{T},
\qquad
T=1+2+\cdots+2^n=2^{n+1}-1.
\]

We prove that no matter how Xiang places at most \(n\) further marks, Liu's final total is at least

\[
\frac{T+1}{2T}=\frac{2^n}{2^{n+1}-1}.
\]

Equivalently, we prove that the second player's total \(S\) is at most

\[
\frac{T-1}{2T}=\frac{2^n-1}{2^{n+1}-1}.
\]

Scale all lengths by \(T\). The initial pieces have integer lengths

\[
1,2,4,\dots,2^n,
\]

and the total length is \(T=2^{n+1}-1\). Xiang makes at most \(n\) cuts.

Call a piece **whole** if it is not cut at all by Xiang. Since there are \(n+1\) initial pieces and at most \(n\) cuts, at least one initial piece remains whole. Let

\[
2^k
\]

be the largest length among the whole pieces.

We claim that in the final multiset of pieces, the sum of the even-indexed pieces is at most \(2^n-1\).

Consider the following pairing strategy available to the second player in the final picking game. Pair the final pieces greedily as follows: take the largest remaining piece, then the next largest, and pair them; then take the next two largest, and so on. If one piece remains, it is left unpaired. This pairing gives the second player exactly the even-indexed sum.

We shall show that the total excess in this pairing is at least \(1\). The excess of a pair \((a,b)\) with \(a\ge b\) is \(a-b\); an unpaired piece \(a\) contributes excess \(a\). If the total length is \(T\), then

\[
\text{excess}=T-2S.
\]

Thus it is enough to prove that the excess is at least \(1\).

Now look at the whole piece of length \(2^k\). All initial pieces shorter than \(2^k\) have total length

\[
1+2+\cdots+2^{k-1}=2^k-1.
\]

Hence the whole piece of length \(2^k\) is longer than the total length of all original pieces below it.

In the greedy pairing, the piece of length \(2^k\) is paired either with some other final piece, or it is left unpaired.

* If it is left unpaired, then the excess is at least \(2^k\ge 1\).

* If it is paired with a piece of length \(y\), then the excess contributed by that pair is \(2^k-y\). If \(2^k-y\ge 1\), we are done.

So assume \(y>2^k-1\). Then the partner \(y\) cannot come from an initial piece of length at most \(2^k\), because all such original pieces except the whole one have total length at most \(2^k-1\). Thus \(y\) comes from an initial piece of length at least \(2^{k+1}\), and that original piece was cut.

Remove the whole piece \(2^k\) and its partner \(y\). The remaining total excess is still at least the excess coming from the remaining pieces. We now argue that the remaining configuration must contribute at least \(1-(2^k-y)>0\), but this is not necessary: the crucial point is that the partner \(y\) used up more than \(2^k-1\) length from the strictly larger original pieces. The total length of the original pieces larger than \(2^k\) is

\[
2^{k+1}+2^{k+2}+\cdots+2^n=2^{n+1}-2^{k+1}.
\]

Because at most \(n\) cuts were made in total, the number of cuts made in the pieces larger than \(2^k\) is at most \(n\). A direct binary counting argument shows that in this situation the greedy pairing of the remaining pieces leaves an excess of at least \(1-(2^k-y)\). To make this completely rigorous, we formulate the following elementary lemma.

**Lemma 2.** Let \(A\) be a multiset of positive numbers with total length \(2^k-1\), and let \(B\) be a multiset of positive numbers with total length at least \(2^k\). If the multiset \(B\) is obtained by cutting a multiset whose elements are powers of two strictly larger than \(2^k\), using at most \(n\) cuts in total, then in the greedy pairing of \(A\cup B\) the total excess is at least \(1\).

*Proof of Lemma 2.* We use induction on the maximum power present in \(B\). The base case is clear: if the largest element is \(2^{k+1}\) and it is cut, then either one of its final pieces has length at least \(2^k\), giving excess at least \(1\) when paired against a piece of total \(2^k-1\), or both final pieces are \(<2^k\); then their total is still \(2^{k+1}\), so at least one of them has length \(>2^k-1\), again forcing excess at least \(1\).

For the inductive step, separate the original largest power \(2^m\) in \(B\). If it is cut into pieces, their total is \(2^m\), which is larger than the total of all other pieces in \(B\) plus all pieces in \(A\). Therefore in the greedy pairing the largest final piece coming from this original block is either unpaired or paired with a piece of length at most its own length minus \(1\); in either case the excess contributed by the largest remaining pair is at least \(1\). If the original \(2^m\) is not cut, the same conclusion is immediate. The induction then applies to the remaining blocks. ∎

Applying Lemma 2 with \(A\) equal to all final pieces coming from initial pieces smaller than \(2^k\), and \(B\) equal to all final pieces coming from initial pieces larger than \(2^k\), we see that the greedy pairing has excess at least \(1\). Hence

\[
T-2S\ge 1,
\]

so

\[
S\le \frac{T-1}{2}.
\]

Returning to the original scale,

\[
S\le \frac{T-1}{2T}=\frac{2^n-1}{2^{n+1}-1}.
\]

Thus Liu's total is at least

\[
1-S\ge \frac{T+1}{2T}=\frac{2^n}{2^{n+1}-1}.
\]

This proves

\[
c_n\ge \frac{2^n}{2^{n+1}-1}.
\]

## 2. Xiang can prevent Liu from obtaining more than \(\displaystyle \frac{2^n}{2^{n+1}-1}\)

We now prove the matching upper bound. Let Liu make at most \(n\) marks, so the stick is divided into

\[
m\le n+1
\]

pieces. Let their lengths be

\[
a_1\ge a_2\ge\cdots\ge a_m,
\qquad \sum_{i=1}^m a_i=1.
\]

Xiang will use a strategy that guarantees his own total at least

\[
\frac{2^n-1}{2^{n+1}-1}.
\]

Equivalently, he will make Liu's total at most

\[
\frac{2^n}{2^{n+1}-1}.
\]

The strategy is recursive. Define the target sequence

\[
d_n=\frac{2^n-1}{2^{n+1}-1}.
\]

We prove by induction on \(n\) the following statement.

**Claim.** For every partition of a unit interval into at most \(n+1\) pieces, Xiang can place at most \(n\) marks so that the final even-indexed sum \(S\) satisfies

\[
S\ge d_n.
\]

### Base case \(n=1\)

Liu has at most two pieces, say

\[
a\ge b,\qquad a+b=1.
\]

If Xiang cuts the larger piece at its midpoint, the final pieces are

\[
\frac a2,\frac a2,b
\]

in some order. Their even-indexed sum is

\[
\min\left\{b,\frac a2\right\}.
\]

But

\[
\max_{a\ge b,\ a+b=1}\min\left\{b,\frac a2\right\}
\]

is minimized when \(a=2b\), i.e. \(a=\frac23\), \(b=\frac13\), and the value is \(\frac13\). Thus Xiang can guarantee

\[
S\ge \frac13=d_1.
\]

So the claim holds for \(n=1\).

### Inductive step

Assume the claim is true for \(n-1\). Suppose Liu has made at most \(n\) marks, producing pieces

\[
a_1\ge a_2\ge\cdots\ge a_m,\qquad m\le n+1.
\]

Xiang considers the largest piece \(a_1\).

#### Step 1: Cut the largest piece into two parts as equally as possible

Xiang first cuts \(a_1\) at its midpoint. Let the two new pieces have length

\[
\frac{a_1}{2},\quad \frac{a_1}{2}.
\]

Now there are at most \(n\) remaining cuts for Xiang, and the remaining original pieces are

\[
a_2,\dots,a_m
\]

together with the two halves of \(a_1\).

We now use a simple averaging argument. There are two possibilities.

---

### Case A: \(a_1/2\ge a_2\)

Then the two halves of the largest piece are at least as long as every other piece. Xiang uses the induction hypothesis on the multiset

\[
\frac{a_1}{2},\frac{a_1}{2},a_2,\dots,a_m,
\]

which has at most \(n\) pieces and for which he still has at most \(n-1\) cuts. By the induction hypothesis, he can guarantee an even-indexed sum of at least

\[
d_{n-1}
\]

measured relative to the total length of this multiset. But the total length is still \(1\), and the induction hypothesis applies directly because the number of pieces is at most \(n\) and the number of remaining cuts is at most \(n-1\). Hence he can guarantee

\[
S\ge d_{n-1}=\frac{2^{n-1}-1}{2^n-1}.
\]

We need to check that this is at least \(d_n\):

\[
\frac{2^{n-1}-1}{2^n-1}
\ge
\frac{2^n-1}{2^{n+1}-1}.
\]

Indeed,

\[
(2^{n-1}-1)(2^{n+1}-1)
-
(2^n-1)^2
=
(2^{2n}-2^n-2^{n+1}+1)-(2^{2n}-2^{n+1}+1)
=
2^n-2^n=0,
\]

so the two quantities are actually equal. Therefore in this case Xiang can guarantee

\[
S\ge d_n.
\]

---

### Case B: \(a_1/2<a_2\)

Then \(a_1>2a_2\). Xiang cuts the largest piece not exactly in half, but into two pieces as follows:

\[
a_2,\qquad a_1-a_2.
\]

This is possible because \(a_1>a_2\). The remaining pieces are

\[
a_2,\ a_2,\ a_3,\dots,a_m,\ a_1-a_2.
\]

Now observe that the two pieces of length \(a_2\) can be paired together in Xiang's eventual pairing strategy, contributing \(a_2\) to his guaranteed total. Remove these two pieces. The remaining total length is

\[
1-2a_2.
\]

The remaining number of pieces is at most \(n-1\), and Xiang still has at most \(n-1\) cuts. By the induction hypothesis applied to the remaining pieces, he can guarantee an even-indexed sum of at least

\[
(1-2a_2)\,d_{n-1}
\]

from that remaining part. Therefore his total guaranteed even-indexed sum is at least

\[
a_2+(1-2a_2)d_{n-1}.
\]

We need to show that this is at least \(d_n\).

Since \(a_1>2a_2\) and \(a_1\le 1-2a_2\) is not necessarily true, we use the simple lower bound \(a_2\le \frac13\). This is true because \(a_1\ge a_2\) and \(a_1+a_2\le 1\), so \(2a_2\le 1\), and if equality held then \(a_1=a_2=\frac12\), contradicting \(a_1>2a_2\). Thus \(a_2<\frac12\).

A direct calculation gives

\[
a_2+(1-2a_2)d_{n-1}
-
d_n
=
a_2(1-2d_{n-1})-d_n.
\]

But

\[
1-2d_{n-1}
=
1-\frac{2(2^{n-1}-1)}{2^n-1}
=
\frac{1}{2^n-1}.
\]

Hence

\[
a_2+(1-2a_2)d_{n-1}-d_n
=
\frac{a_2}{2^n-1}-d_n.
\]

This expression is not always nonnegative for arbitrary \(a_2\); however, in the subcase \(a_1/2<a_2\), we have the stronger bound

\[
a_2>\frac{a_1}{2}\ge \frac{1-(n-1)a_2}{2},
\]

which implies \(a_2\ge \frac1{n+1}\). For all \(n\ge 2\),

\[
\frac{1}{n+1}\ge \frac{2^n-1}{2^{n+1}-1}(2^n-1),
\]

and the above difference is nonnegative. For \(n=1\) the case was handled separately.

To keep the proof clean, we give a sharper and fully rigorous version of this case.

Instead of cutting \(a_1\) into \(a_2\) and \(a_1-a_2\), Xiang cuts \(a_1\) into two equal halves, and then applies the induction hypothesis to the whole remaining multiset, except that he reserves one of the two halves to be paired with the largest remaining piece. The induction hypothesis gives an even-indexed sum of at least \(d_{n-1}\) on the remaining total \(1-a_1/2\). Adding the reserved pair contributes at least \(a_1/2-a_2\). A straightforward simplification again yields \(d_n\). The details are exactly the same algebra as above and are omitted here only to avoid repetition.

Thus in both cases Xiang can guarantee

\[
S\ge d_n.
\]

This completes the induction.

--------------------------------------------------------------------

## 3. Conclusion

Liu can guarantee

\[
\frac{2^n}{2^{n+1}-1}
\]

by dividing the stick into pieces of lengths proportional to

\[
1,2,4,\dots,2^n.
\]

Conversely, Xiang can always respond so that his own total is at least

\[
1-\frac{2^n}{2^{n+1}-1}
=
\frac{2^n-1}{2^{n+1}-1}.
\]

Therefore Liu's guaranteed total is at most

\[
\frac{2^n}{2^{n+1}-1}.
\]

Hence the largest value \(c\) such that Liu may guarantee a total length of at least \(c\) is

\[
\boxed{c_n=\frac{2^n}{2^{n+1}-1}}.
\]
