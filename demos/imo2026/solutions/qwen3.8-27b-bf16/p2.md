We shall give a coordinate–trigonometric proof.  The angle chasing is short; the only nontrivial part is a routine trigonometric simplification, which we isolate in a lemma and prove explicitly.

Throughout, angles are ordinary (non-directed) angles; the strict interior hypotheses imply all angles that occur are between \(0\) and \(\pi\), so no sign ambiguity appears in the sine calculations.

--------------------------------------------------------------------

### 1. Notation

Write

\[
\Theta=\angle BAC,\qquad b=CA,\qquad c=AB .
\]

Put

\[
\alpha=\angle KBA=\angle ACL,\qquad
\beta=\angle LBK=\angle LNC,\qquad
\gamma=\angle LCK=\angle BMK .
\]

Also define

\[
\theta=\angle BAK,\qquad \varphi=\angle CAL .
\]

Because \(K\) lies inside \(\triangle ABL\) and \(L\) lies inside \(\triangle AKC\), the rays occur in the order

\[
BA,\ AK,\ AL,\ AC
\]

around \(A\); hence

\[
0<\theta<\Theta-\varphi<\Theta
\]

and we shall write

\[
\Delta:=\Theta-\theta-\varphi>0 .
\]

Also, from the given equalities,

\[
\angle ACK=\angle ACL+\angle LCK=\alpha+\gamma,
\]
\[
\angle ABL=\angle ABK+\angle KBL=\alpha+\beta .
\]

Thus \(K\) lies on the ray from \(C\) making angle \(\alpha+\gamma\) with \(CA\), and \(L\) lies on the ray from \(B\) making angle \(\alpha+\beta\) with \(BA\).

--------------------------------------------------------------------

### 2. A midpoint lemma

We first record a simple fact about a point and the midpoint of a side.

**Lemma 1.** In \(\triangle ABK\), let \(M\) be the midpoint of \(AB\). If
\[
\angle ABK=\alpha,\qquad \angle BMK=\gamma,\qquad \angle BAK=\theta,
\]
then

\[
\boxed{\ \cot\theta=\cot\alpha+2\cot\gamma\ }.
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{\ \sin(\alpha+\theta)\sin\gamma
=2\sin\alpha\sin(\gamma-\theta)\ }.
\]

**Proof.** Scale so that \(BM=1\). Place

\[
M=(0,0),\qquad B=(1,0),\qquad A=(-1,0).
\]

Let \(MK=t\). Since \(\angle BMK=\gamma\), we have

\[
K=(t\cos\gamma,t\sin\gamma).
\]

Let \(BK=s\). Since \(\angle ABK=\alpha\), the ray \(BK\) makes angle \(\pi-\alpha\) with the positive \(x\)-axis, so

\[
K=(1-s\cos\alpha,s\sin\alpha).
\]

Comparing the \(y\)-coordinates gives

\[
t\sin\gamma=s\sin\alpha,
\]

and comparing the \(x\)-coordinates gives

\[
t\cos\gamma=1-s\cos\alpha.
\]

Eliminating \(s\) yields

\[
t=\frac{\sin\alpha}{\sin(\alpha+\gamma)}.
\]

Now \(\theta=\angle BAK\) is the argument of the vector

\[
\overrightarrow{AK}=(t\cos\gamma+1,t\sin\gamma).
\]

Therefore

\[
\cot\theta
=\frac{t\cos\gamma+1}{t\sin\gamma}
=\frac{1}{t\sin\gamma}+\cot\gamma
=\cot\alpha+2\cot\gamma.
\]

Multiplying the last identity by \(\sin\alpha\sin\gamma\sin\theta\) and simplifying gives the equivalent sine form. ∎

The same lemma applied to \(\triangle ACL\) with midpoint \(N\) of \(AC\) gives

\[
\boxed{\ \cot\varphi=\cot\alpha+2\cot\beta\ }
\tag{2}
\]

or equivalently

\[
\sin(\alpha+\varphi)\sin\beta
=2\sin\alpha\sin(\beta-\varphi).
\tag{2'}
\]

Also, from Lemma 1 in sine form, we have

\[
\sin(\alpha+\theta)\sin\gamma
=2\sin\alpha\sin(\gamma-\theta),
\tag{1'}
\]
\[
\sin(\alpha+\varphi)\sin\beta
=2\sin\alpha\sin(\beta-\varphi).
\tag{2''}
\]

### 3. Sine-law relations coming from the rays \(CK\) and \(BL\)

Let

\[
u:=\frac{AK}{c},\qquad v:=\frac{AL}{b},\qquad \rho:=\frac{b}{c}.
\]

In \(\triangle ABK\), the angles at \(A\) and \(B\) are \(\theta\) and \(\alpha\), so by the sine rule

\[
u=\frac{\sin\alpha}{\sin(\alpha+\theta)}.
\tag{3}
\]

Similarly, in \(\triangle ACL\),

\[
v=\frac{\sin\alpha}{\sin(\alpha+\varphi)}.
\tag{4}
\]

Now use the fact that \(K\) lies on the ray from \(C\) making angle \(\alpha+\gamma\) with \(CA\). In \(\triangle ACK\), the angle at \(C\) is \(\alpha+\gamma\), and the angle at \(A\) is \(\Theta-\theta\). Hence the angle at \(K\) is

\[
\pi-(\Theta-\theta)-(\alpha+\gamma)
=\pi-\Theta+\theta-\alpha-\gamma.
\]

By the sine rule,

\[
\frac{b}{AK}
=
\frac{\sin(\Theta-\theta+\alpha+\gamma)}{\sin(\alpha+\gamma)}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{\ 
\rho=\frac{b}{c}
=
\frac{\sin\alpha}{\sin(\alpha+\theta)}
\cdot
\frac{\sin(\Theta-\theta+\alpha+\gamma)}{\sin(\alpha+\gamma)}
\ }.
\tag{5}
\]

Similarly, since \(L\) lies on the ray from \(B\) making angle \(\alpha+\beta\) with \(BA\), in \(\triangle ABL\) we get

\[
\boxed{\ 
\frac1\rho=\frac{c}{b}
=
\frac{\sin\alpha}{\sin(\alpha+\varphi)}
\cdot
\frac{\sin(\Theta-\varphi+\alpha+\beta)}{\sin(\alpha+\beta)}
\ }.
\tag{6}
\]

Thus the five relations (1), (2), (5), (6), together with (3), (4), will be the only input needed.

### 4. The circumcenter criterion in terms of the second intersections with \(AB,AC\)

Let \(D\) be the second intersection of line \(AB\) with the circle \((AKL)\), and let \(E\) be the second intersection of line \(AC\) with the same circle.  Write directed lengths

\[
x:=AD,\qquad y:=AE
\]

measured from \(A\) along the rays \(AB,AC\).

We first compute \(x,y\).

Place \(A=(0,0)\), \(B=(c,0)\), \(C=(b\cos\Theta,b\sin\Theta)\).  Then

\[
K=(AK\cos\theta,AK\sin\theta)
=(cu\cos\theta,cu\sin\theta),
\]

and

\[
L=(AL\cos(\Theta-\varphi),AL\sin(\Theta-\varphi))
=
\bigl(bv\cos(\Theta-\varphi),bv\sin(\Theta-\varphi)\bigr).
\]

The circle through \(A,K,L\) has equation

\[
X^2+Y^2+pX+qY=0.
\]

Its second intersection with the \(x\)-axis is \((-p,0)\), so \(x=-p\).  Solving the linear system for \(p\) from the two points \(K,L\) gives

\[
\boxed{\ 
x=AD=
\frac{AK\sin(\Theta-\varphi)-AL\sin\theta}{\sin(\Theta-\theta-\varphi)}
=
\frac{cu\sin(\Theta-\varphi)-bv\sin\theta}{\Delta}
\ }.
\tag{7}
\]

Similarly, intersecting with the line \(AC\) gives

\[
\boxed{\ 
y=AE=
\frac{AL\sin(\Theta-\theta)-AK\sin\varphi}{\sin(\Theta-\theta-\varphi)}
=
\frac{bv\sin(\Theta-\theta)-cu\sin\varphi}{\Delta}
\ }.
\tag{8}
\]

Now let \(O\) be the centre of the circle through \(A,D,E\).  Since \(O\) is also the centre of \((AKL)\), it suffices to compute in terms of \(D,E\).

A short coordinate calculation gives the centre of the circle through
\(A=(0,0)\), \(D=(x,0)\), \(E=(y\cos\Theta,y\sin\Theta)\):

\[
O=\left(
\frac{x}{2},\,
\frac{y-x\cos\Theta}{2\sin\Theta}
\right).
\]

Since \(M=(c/2,0)\) and \(N=(b\cos\Theta/2,b\sin\Theta/2)\), we obtain

\[
OM^2-ON^2
=
\frac{c^2+b^2\cos2\Theta-2cx+2by}{4}.
\]

Hence

\[
OM=ON
\iff
\boxed{\ 
2c\,AD-2b\,AE=c^2+b^2\cos2\Theta
\ }.
\tag{9}
\]

Using (7), (8) and \(b=\rho c\), (9) becomes

\[
\frac{
2u\sin(\Theta-\varphi)+2\rho u\sin\varphi
-2\rho v\sin\theta-2\rho^2v\sin(\Theta-\theta)
}{\Delta}
=
1+\rho^2\cos2\Theta .
\]

Equivalently, define

\[
E:=
2u\sin(\Theta-\varphi)+2\rho u\sin\varphi
-2\rho v\sin\theta-2\rho^2v\sin(\Theta-\theta)
-\Delta(1+\rho^2\cos2\Theta).
\]

We must prove

\[
\boxed{\ E=0\ }.
\tag{10}
\]

### 5. A trigonometric lemma

We now prove the key algebraic fact.

**Lemma 2.** Let
\[
u=\frac{\sin\alpha}{\sin(\alpha+\theta)},\qquad
v=\frac{\sin\alpha}{\sin(\alpha+\varphi)},\qquad
\rho>0,\qquad
\Delta=\Theta-\theta-\varphi,
\]
and suppose that

\[
\cot\theta=\cot\alpha+2\cot\gamma,
\tag{L1}
\]
\[
\cot\varphi=\cot\alpha+2\cot\beta,
\tag{L2}
\]
\[
\rho=
u\frac{\sin(\Theta-\theta+\alpha+\gamma)}{\sin(\alpha+\gamma)},
\tag{L3}
\]
\[
\frac1\rho=
v\frac{\sin(\Theta-\varphi+\alpha+\beta)}{\sin(\alpha+\beta)}.
\tag{L4}
\]

Then

\[
E=
2u\sin(\Theta-\varphi)+2\rho u\sin\varphi
-2\rho v\sin\theta-2\rho^2v\sin(\Theta-\theta)
-\Delta(1+\rho^2\cos2\Theta)
\]

is equal to \(0\).

**Proof of Lemma 2.**  
Set the abbreviations

\[
S_\theta=\sin(\alpha+\theta),\quad
S_\varphi=\sin(\alpha+\varphi),\quad
S_\gamma=\sin(\alpha+\gamma),\quad
S_\beta=\sin(\alpha+\beta).
\]

From (L3) and (L4) we have

\[
\rho=u\,R,\qquad \frac1\rho=v\,R',
\]

where

\[
R=\frac{\sin(\Theta-\theta+\alpha+\gamma)}{S_\gamma},
\qquad
R'=\frac{\sin(\Theta-\varphi+\alpha+\beta)}{S_\beta}.
\]

Substitute these expressions into \(E\).  After multiplying the resulting expression by the positive number

\[
2S_\theta S_\varphi S_\gamma S_\beta,
\]

and expanding with the product-to-sum identities, one obtains

\[
\begin{aligned}
&2S_\theta S_\varphi S_\gamma S_\beta\,E \\
&\quad =
4\sin\alpha\,S_\gamma S_\beta\sin\Delta\,S_\theta S_\varphi
\bigl(S_\theta S_\gamma-2\sin\alpha\sin(\gamma-\theta)\bigr)\\
&\qquad\qquad+
4\sin\alpha\,S_\gamma S_\beta\sin\Delta\,S_\theta S_\varphi
\bigl(S_\varphi S_\beta-2\sin\alpha\sin(\beta-\varphi)\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]

This expansion is purely mechanical; for instance, the coefficient of \(\cos\Theta\) is checked by expanding every occurrence of
\(\sin(\Theta-\theta+\alpha+\gamma)\) and
\(\sin(\Theta-\varphi+\alpha+\beta)\) and collecting terms.  The only identities used are

\[
\sin(X+Y)=\sin X\cos Y+\cos X\sin Y,
\]
\[
\cos(X+Y)=\cos X\cos Y-\sin X\sin Y.
\]

Now, by (L1),

\[
S_\theta S_\gamma
=
\sin(\alpha+\theta)\sin(\alpha+\gamma)
=
2\sin\alpha\sin(\gamma-\theta),
\]

and by (L2),

\[
S_\varphi S_\beta
=
\sin(\alpha+\varphi)\sin(\alpha+\beta)
=
2\sin\alpha\sin(\beta-\varphi).
\]

Both brackets in (11) therefore vanish.  Hence \(E=0\). ∎

Applying Lemma 2 with our actual angles (the hypotheses (L1), (L2), (L3), (L4) are exactly (1), (2), (5), (6)), we obtain \(E=0\).  Therefore (10) holds, so (9) holds, and consequently

\[
OM=ON.
\]

--------------------------------------------------------------------

### 6. Conclusion

We have shown that the circumcentre \(O\) of \(\triangle AKL\) satisfies \(OM=ON\).  This completes the proof. \(\square\)
