We prove that the only solutions are
\[
\boxed{f(x)=x+c\quad (x>0)}
\]
where \(c\) is an arbitrary nonnegative real constant.

---

## 1. Reformatting the condition

For \(x,y>0\), the given inequality is equivalent, after squaring, to
\[
2x^2+2f(y)^2\ge (f(x)+y)^2
\tag{1}
\]
and
\[
(f(x)+y)^2\ge 4x f(y).
\tag{2}
\]

Define
\[
h(x):=f(x)-x\qquad (x>0).
\]
We shall prove that \(h\) is constant and nonnegative.

---

## 2. Invariance of \(h\) along the orbit of \(f\)

Take any \(x>0\), and put \(u=f(x)\). Apply the original inequality to the pair \((u,x)\). Since \(f(x)=u\), we get
\[
\sqrt{\frac{u^2+u^2}{2}}
=
u
\ge
\frac{f(u)+x}{2}
\ge
\sqrt{u\cdot u}
=
u.
\]
Hence equality holds throughout, so
\[
\frac{f(u)+x}{2}=u,
\]
i.e.
\[
f(f(x))+x=2f(x).
\]
Therefore
\[
f(f(x))-f(x)=f(x)-x,
\]
which means
\[
h(f(x))=h(x)
\tag{3}
\]
for every \(x>0\).

Equivalently,
\[
f(f(x))=2f(x)-x.
\tag{4}
\]

---

## 3. \(h(x)\ge 0\) for all \(x\)

Suppose, for contradiction, that for some \(x>0\),
\[
h(x)=a<0.
\]
Then \(f(x)=x+a\). By (3), \(h(f(x))=a\), and iterating gives
\[
f^n(x)=x+na
\]
for every integer \(n\ge 0\) for which \(x+na>0\).

Since \(a<0\), there is a largest integer \(N\ge 0\) such that
\[
x+Na>0.
\]
Then \(x+Na>0\), so \(f(x+Na)\) must be positive. But by the formula above,
\[
f(x+Na)=x+(N+1)a\le 0,
\]
a contradiction.

Thus
\[
h(x)=f(x)-x\ge 0
\tag{5}
\]
for every \(x>0\).

---

## 4. Arithmetic-ray structure

Fix \(x>0\), and write
\[
h(x)=a\ge 0.
\]

### Forward direction

From \(h(x)=a\), we have \(f(x)=x+a\). By (3), \(h(x+a)=a\). Inductively, for every \(n\ge 0\),
\[
h(x+na)=a
\]
and therefore
\[
f(x+na)=x+(n+1)a.
\tag{6}
\]

Thus the forward orbit of \(x\) is the arithmetic progression
\[
x,\;x+a,\;x+2a,\;x+3a,\dots
\]
on which \(h\) is constant equal to \(a\).

### Backward direction

Assume \(a>0\) and \(x>a\). Apply the original inequality to the pair \((x,x-a)\). Since
\[
f(x)=x+a,
\]
the middle term is
\[
\frac{f(x)+(x-a)}2=\frac{x+a+x-a}{2}=x.
\]
The inequality becomes
\[
\sqrt{\frac{x^2+f(x-a)^2}{2}}
\ge x
\ge
\sqrt{x\,f(x-a)}.
\]
From the left inequality,
\[
\frac{x^2+f(x-a)^2}{2}\ge x^2
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
f(x-a)\ge x.
\]
From the right inequality,
\[
x^2\ge x\,f(x-a)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
f(x-a)\le x.
\]
Hence
\[
f(x-a)=x,
\]
and so
\[
h(x-a)=f(x-a)-(x-a)=x-(x-a)=a.
\tag{7}
\]

Thus, whenever \(a>0\), we can subtract \(a\) as long as the number stays positive, and \(h\) remains equal to \(a\). In particular, for every \(x\) with \(h(x)=a>0\), there is a unique minimal positive element
\[
r\in(0,a]
\]
in its orbit, and the whole positive ray
\[
r+na\qquad(n=0,1,2,\dots)
\]
satisfies
\[
h(r+na)=a.
\tag{8}
\]

---

## 5. Two different positive values of \(h\) cannot occur

Assume that there exist \(p,q>0\) such that
\[
h(p)=a>0,\qquad h(q)=b>0,
\]
with \(a\ne b\).

By (6), for every \(n\ge 0\),
\[
X_n:=p+na
\]
satisfies \(h(X_n)=a\), and \(X_n\to\infty\).

Similarly, for every \(m\ge 0\),
\[
q+mb
\]
satisfies \(h(q+mb)=b\).

For all sufficiently large \(n\), \(X_n\ge q\). Choose \(m=m(n)\) so that
\[
q+mb\le X_n<q+(m+1)b.
\]
Set
\[
Y_n:=q+m(n)b,
\qquad
\delta_n:=X_n-Y_n.
\]
Then
\[
0\le \delta_n<b
\tag{9}
\]
and \(X_n\to\infty\).

Now compute the two squared differences for the pair \((X,Y)\), where
\[
h(X)=a,\qquad h(Y)=b,
\]
and write \(\delta=X-Y\). Since
\[
f(X)=X+a,\qquad f(Y)=Y+b=X-\delta+b,
\]
we have

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal U(X,Y)
&:=2X^2+2f(Y)^2-(f(X)+Y)^2\\
&=4X(b-a)+2(b-\delta)^2-(a-\delta)^2,
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
\]
and
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal L(X,Y)
&:=(f(X)+Y)^2-4Xf(Y)\\
&=4X(a-b)+(a-\delta)^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]

The given condition requires
\[
\mathcal U(X,Y)\ge 0
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\mathcal L(X,Y)\ge 0
\]
for all positive \(X,Y\).

Because of (9), the quantities \(2(b-\delta_n)^2-(a-\delta_n)^2\) and \((a-\delta_n)^2\) remain bounded as \(n\to\infty\).

- If \(a>b\), then by (10),
  \[
  \mathcal U(X_n,Y_n)
  =-4X_n(a-b)+O(1)\to -\infty,
  \]
  impossible.

- If \(a<b\), then by (11),
  \[
  \mathcal L(X_n,Y_n)
  =4X_n(a-b)+O(1)\to -\infty,
  \]
  also impossible.

Therefore two distinct positive values of \(h\) cannot occur.

---

## 6. Zero cannot coexist with a positive value

Now suppose
\[
h(q)=0
\]
for some \(q>0\), and suppose there is \(p>0\) with
\[
h(p)=a>0.
\]

Let \(r\in(0,a]\) be the minimal positive element of the \(a\)-ray containing \(p\). By (8),
\[
h(r+na)=a
\]
for all \(n\ge 0\).

Choose \(n\) such that
\[
q+a\le X:=r+na<q+a+a.
\]
This is possible because the ray \(r+na\) has step \(a\) and tends to \(+\infty\), while \(r\le a<q+a\).

Write
\[
X=q+a+\varepsilon,
\qquad 0\le \varepsilon<a.
\]

Since \(h(X)=a\) and \(h(q)=0\), we have
\[
f(X)=X+a,\qquad f(q)=q.
\]
Compute the upper squared difference:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal U(X,q)
&=2X^2+2q^2-(X+q+a)^2\\
&=2(q+a+\varepsilon)^2+2q^2-(2q+a+\varepsilon)^2\\
&=\varepsilon^2-4aq-2a^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
Because \(\varepsilon<a\),
\[
\mathcal U(X,q)
<a^2-4aq-2a^2
=-a^2-4aq<0,
\]
contradicting (1).

Hence \(h\) cannot take both the value \(0\) and a positive value.

---

## 7. Conclusion: \(h\) is constant

From (5), \(h(x)\ge 0\) for all \(x>0\).

Sections 5 and 6 show that \(h\) can take at most one value: either it is identically \(0\), or it is identically some positive constant \(c\). In either case,
\[
h(x)=c\ge 0
\]
for all \(x>0\). Therefore
\[
f(x)=x+c
\]
for some constant \(c\ge 0\).

---

## 8. Verification

Let \(c\ge 0\) and define
\[
f(x)=x+c\qquad(x>0).
\]
Then \(f(x)>0\) for all \(x>0\). For \(x,y>0\), set \(B=y+c=f(y)\). We have
\[
f(x)=x+c.
\]

Now compute:
\[
\frac{x^2+f(y)^2}{2}
-
\frac{(f(x)+y)^2}{4}
=
\frac{2x^2+2(y+c)^2-(x+y+c)^2}{4}
=
\frac{(x-y-c)^2}{4}
\ge 0.
\]
Thus
\[
\sqrt{\frac{x^2+f(y)^2}{2}}
\ge
\frac{f(x)+y}{2}.
\]

Also,
\[
\frac{(f(x)+y)^2}{4}
-
x f(y)
=
\frac{(x+y+c)^2}{4}-x(y+c)
=
\frac{(x-y-c)^2}{4}
\ge 0.
\]
Thus
\[
\frac{f(x)+y}{2}
\ge
\sqrt{x f(y)}.
\]

Therefore every function
\[
f(x)=x+c\qquad(c\ge 0)
\]
satisfies the required condition.

Hence the complete set of solutions is
\[
\boxed{f(x)=x+c\quad\text{for some constant }c\ge 0.}
\]
