We claim that the solutions are exactly

\[
\boxed{f(x)=x+c\quad\text{for some fixed }c\ge 0.}
\]

We give a complete proof.

---

## 1. Reformulation of the inequality

For positive numbers, the given condition

\[
\sqrt{\frac{x^{2}+f(y)^{2}}{2}}
\ge \frac{f(x)+y}{2}
\ge \sqrt{x f(y)}
\]

is equivalent to the two squared inequalities

\[
\tag{1}
(f(x)+y)^2\le 2x^2+2f(y)^2
\]

and

\[
\tag{2}
(f(x)+y)^2\ge 4x f(y).
\]

Both inequalities must hold for all \(x,y>0\).

---

## 2. A basic identity forced by the condition

Apply the original inequality to the ordered pair \((x,y)=(f(x),x)\).  
Then

\[
\sqrt{\frac{f(x)^2+f(x)^2}{2}}=f(x),
\]

and

\[
\sqrt{f(x)\cdot f(x)}=f(x).
\]

The condition therefore gives

\[
f(x)\ge \frac{f(f(x))+x}{2}\ge f(x).
\]

Hence

\[
\frac{f(f(x))+x}{2}=f(x),
\]

so for every \(x>0\),

\[
\boxed{f(f(x))=2f(x)-x.}\tag{3}
\]

Define

\[
g(x):=f(x)-x.
\]

From (3),

\[
g(f(x))=f(f(x))-f(x)=f(x)-x=g(x).
\tag{4}
\]

We next show that \(g(x)\ge 0\) for all \(x>0\). Suppose \(g(x)<0\). Then by induction from (4),

\[
f^n(x)=x+ng(x)
\]

for every integer \(n\ge 0\). Since \(g(x)<0\), this becomes negative for all sufficiently large \(n\), contradicting that \(f^n(x)\in \mathbb R_{>0}\). Therefore

\[
g(x)\ge 0\quad\text{for all }x>0.
\tag{5}
\]

Thus every \(x\) either satisfies \(f(x)=x\), or \(f(x)=x+d(x)\) with \(d(x):=g(x)>0\).

---

## 3. Positive values of \(g\) are all equal

We prove that if \(g(x),g(y)>0\), then \(g(x)=g(y)\).

Let

\[
a:=g(x)>0,\qquad b:=g(y)>0.
\]

For integers \(n,m\ge 0\), put

\[
X=x+na,\qquad Y=y+mb.
\]

By (4), we have

\[
g(X)=a,\qquad g(Y)=b.
\]

Thus \(f(X)=X+a\) and \(f(Y)=Y+b\).

Apply the squared inequalities (1) and (2) to the pair \((X,Y)\). The left inequality becomes

\[
\Delta_L:=2X^2+2(Y+b)^2-(X+Y+a)^2\ge 0.
\tag{6}
\]

The right inequality becomes

\[
\Delta_R:=(X+Y+a)^2-4X(Y+b)\ge 0.
\tag{7}
\]

We expand \(\Delta_L\) and \(\Delta_R\) in terms of \(n,m\). A direct expansion gives

\[
\Delta_L=(an-bm)^2+2a(x-y-a)n+2b(y+2b-x-a)m+C_L,
\tag{8}
\]

and

\[
\Delta_R=(an-bm)^2+2a(x-y+a-2b)n+2b(y-x+a)m+C_R,
\tag{9}
\]

where

\[
C_L=2x^2+2(y+b)^2-(x+y+a)^2,
\]

\[
C_R=(x+y+a)^2-4x(y+b)
\]

are constants independent of \(n,m\).

Now let \(t\to\infty\) and choose

\[
m=t,\qquad n=\left\lfloor \frac{bt}{a}+\frac12\right\rfloor.
\]

Then

\[
n=\frac{bt}{a}+\varepsilon,
\qquad |\varepsilon|\le \frac12,
\]

and hence

\[
an-bt=a\varepsilon,
\qquad |an-bt|\le \frac a2.
\]

Substituting this into (8), we obtain

\[
\Delta_L
=4b(b-a)t+K_L(t),
\]

where the “bounded term” \(K_L(t)\) satisfies

\[
K_L(t)=a^2\varepsilon^2+2a(x-y-a)\varepsilon+C_L,
\]

so \(K_L(t)\) is bounded as \(t\to\infty\). Similarly, substituting into (9) gives

\[
\Delta_R
=4b(a-b)t+K_R(t),
\]

with bounded \(K_R(t)\).

Now:

- If \(a>b\), then \(4b(b-a)t\to -\infty\). Hence for sufficiently large \(t\), \(\Delta_L<0\), contradicting (6).
- If \(b>a\), then \(4b(a-b)t\to -\infty\). Hence for sufficiently large \(t\), \(\Delta_R<0\), contradicting (7).

Therefore we must have

\[
a=b.
\]

Thus:

\[
\boxed{\text{If }g(x)>0\text{ and }g(y)>0,\text{ then }g(x)=g(y).}
\]

---

## 4. Either \(g\equiv 0\) or \(g\equiv d>0\)

Let

\[
P:=\{x>0:g(x)>0\}.
\]

From the previous section, if \(P\neq\varnothing\), then there is a number \(d>0\) such that

\[
g(x)=d\qquad\text{for all }x\in P.
\]

Thus for \(x\in P\),

\[
f(x)=x+d,
\]

and for \(x\notin P\),

\[
g(x)=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad f(x)=x.
\]

We now prove that \(P\) cannot contain some points and miss others. Suppose there exist points where \(g=0\) and points where \(g=d>0\).

Partition \(\mathbb R_{>0}\) into residue classes modulo \(d\). For each \(r\in(0,d]\), define

\[
C_r:=\{r+kd:k=0,1,2,\dots\}.
\]

Inside a fixed class \(C_r\), the set of points of \(P\) must be a tail: indeed, if \(r+kd\in P\), then

\[
f(r+kd)=r+(k+1)d\in P.
\]

So if some point of \(C_r\) belongs to \(P\), then all later points of that class belong to \(P\).

We first show that a class cannot contain both a fixed point \(f(z)=z\) and a shifted point \(f(w)=w+d\).

Indeed, suppose \(u\) and \(v\) are two points with

\[
|v-u|=d,\qquad f(u)=u,\qquad f(v)=v+d.
\]

Apply the left inequality (1) to the ordered pair \((v,u)\). Since \(f(v)=v+d\) and \(f(u)=u\), it becomes

\[
(v+d+u)^2\le 2v^2+2u^2.
\]

But

\[
(v+d+u)^2-2v^2-2u^2
=d^2-(v-u)^2+2d(u+v).
\]

Because \(|v-u|=d\), the left-hand side equals

\[
2d(u+v)>0.
\]

This contradicts the required inequality. Therefore no residue class can contain both a fixed point and a shifted point.

Hence each class \(C_r\) is either entirely outside \(P\), or entirely inside \(P\).

Now suppose there exist two classes: one entirely in \(P\), say \(C_s\), and one entirely outside \(P\), say \(C_r\), with \(r\neq s\). For each \(k\ge0\), take

\[
X_k=s+kd\in P,\qquad Y_k=r+kd\notin P.
\]

Then \(f(X_k)=X_k+d\) and \(f(Y_k)=Y_k\). Apply the left inequality (1) to the ordered pair \((X_k,Y_k)\). It would require

\[
(X_k+Y_k+d)^2\le 2X_k^2+2Y_k^2.
\]

But

\[
(X_k+Y_k+d)^2-2X_k^2-2Y_k^2
=d^2-(X_k-Y_k)^2+2d(X_k+Y_k).
\]

Since \(X_k-Y_k=s-r\) is constant, while \(X_k+Y_k\to\infty\), the right-hand side tends to \(+\infty\). Therefore it is positive for all sufficiently large \(k\), again contradicting the left inequality.

Thus the set \(P\) is either empty or all of \(\mathbb R_{>0}\).

- If \(P=\varnothing\), then \(g\equiv0\), so

\[
f(x)=x\qquad\text{for all }x>0.
\]

- If \(P=\mathbb R_{>0}\), then \(g(x)=d>0\) for all \(x\), so

\[
f(x)=x+d\qquad\text{for all }x>0.
\]

Combining the two cases, the only possible functions are

\[
f(x)=x+c
\]

with \(c\ge0\).

---

## 5. Verification

Conversely, fix \(c\ge0\) and define

\[
f(x)=x+c.
\]

For \(x,y>0\), we have \(f(x)=x+c\) and \(f(y)=y+c\). We check the squared inequalities.

First,

\[
(f(x)+y)^2-4x f(y)
=(x+y+c)^2-4x(y+c).
\]

Expanding,

\[
(x+y+c)^2-4x(y+c)
=(x-y-c)^2\ge0.
\]

Second,

\[
2x^2+2f(y)^2-(f(x)+y)^2
=2x^2+2(y+c)^2-(x+y+c)^2.
\]

Again expanding,

\[
2x^2+2(y+c)^2-(x+y+c)^2
=(x-y-c)^2\ge0.
\]

Thus the two squared inequalities (1) and (2) hold for all \(x,y>0\). Taking square roots gives the original condition.

Therefore all and only the functions

\[
\boxed{f(x)=x+c\qquad(c\ge0)}
\]

satisfy the given condition.
