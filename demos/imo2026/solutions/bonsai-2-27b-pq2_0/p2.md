## 1. A useful reformulation

Let \(\Gamma\) be the circumcircle of \(\triangle AKL\), and let \(O\) be its centre.  
Let \(D\) be the point diametrically opposite to \(A\) on \(\Gamma\).

Because \(O\) is the midpoint of \(AD\) and \(M\) is the midpoint of \(AB\), the segment \(OM\) is a midline in \(\triangle DAB\). Hence

\[
OM \parallel DB,\qquad DB=2\,OM.
\]

Similarly, \(N\) is the midpoint of \(AC\), so in \(\triangle DAC\) we have

\[
ON \parallel DC,\qquad DC=2\,ON.
\]

Therefore the desired equality

\[
OM=ON
\]

is equivalent to

\[
DB=DC.
\]

So it suffices to prove that the point \(D\), antipodal to \(A\) on \((AKL)\), is equidistant from \(B\) and \(C\).

## 3. Translating the angle conditions into real ratios

For two non-zero complex numbers \(u,v\), the equality of the (non-directed) angles between two pairs of lines is equivalent to the reality of the appropriate ratio of complex numbers. Using directed angles modulo \(180^\circ\), the three hypotheses become the following three “real ratio” conditions.

### Condition (i): \(\angle KBA=\angle ACL\)

At \(B\), the two rays are \(BK=b\) and \(BA=a-b\).  
At \(C\), the two rays are \(CA=a-c\) and \(CL=1-c\). Hence

\[
\frac{b(1-c)}{(a-b)(a-c)}\in\mathbb R.
\tag{1}
\]

### Condition (ii): \(\angle LBK=\angle LNC\)

At \(B\), the rays are \(BL=1-b\) and \(BK=b\).  
At \(N\), the rays are \(NL=1-N=\frac{2-a-c}{2}\) and \(NC=c-N=\frac{c-a}{2}\). Thus

\[
\frac{(1-b)b^{-1}}{\frac{2-a-c}{2}\cdot\frac{c-a}{2}}
=
\frac{(b-1)(c-a)}{b(2-a-c)}\in\mathbb R.
\tag{2}
\]

### Condition (iii): \(\angle LCK=\angle BMK\)

At \(C\), the rays are \(CL=1-c\) and \(CK=c\).  
At \(M\), the rays are \(MB=b-M=\frac{b-a}{2}\) and \(MK=K-M=-\frac{a+b}{2}\). Hence

\[
\frac{(1-c)c^{-1}}{\frac{b-a}{2}\cdot\left(-\frac{a+b}{2}\right)}
=
\frac{(c-1)(a+b)}{c(a-b)}\in\mathbb R.
\tag{3}
\]

Thus the three hypotheses are exactly equivalent to the three reality conditions

\[
\boxed{

\frac{(b-1)(c-a)}{b(2-a-c)}\in\mathbb R,

\frac{(c-1)(a+b)}{c(a-b)}\in\mathbb R.

\tag{4}

The strict interior conditions only determine the correct sign/orientation of the directed angles; only the reality of the ratios is needed.

## 4. Translating \(OM=ON\) into a complex identity

Because \(O\) is the centre of \((0,1,a)\), the condition \(OM=ON\) is equivalent to saying that the two points

\[
M=\frac{a+b}{2},\qquad N=\frac{a+c}{2}

\[
P(z)=|z|^2-\operatorname{Re}z-\lambda\operatorname{Im}z.

\[

It remains to show that the right-hand side of (5) vanishes under the reality conditions (4).

## 5. Algebraic verification

Define

\[
R_1=\frac{b(1-c)}{(a-b)(a-c)},\qquad
R_2=\frac{(b-1)(c-a)}{b(2-a-c)},\qquad
R_3=\frac{(c-1)(a+b)}{c(a-b)}.
\]

By (4), \(R_1,R_2,R_3\in\mathbb R\).

\[
4\bigl(P(M)-P(N)\bigr)
=
\frac{2i}{(a-b)(a-c)(b-1)(c-1)(2-a-c)}
\left(
b(1-c)(c-a)(b-1)R_1
+
(b-1)(c-a)(c-1)(a+b)R_2
+
(c-1)(a+b)(b-1)(1-c)R_3
\right).
\tag{6}
\]

\[
4\bigl(P(M)-P(N)\bigr)
=
\frac{2}{(a-b)(a-c)(b-1)(c-1)(2-a-c)}
\left(
\operatorname{Im}R_1\cdot A
+
\operatorname{Im}R_2\cdot B
+
\operatorname{Im}R_3\cdot C
\right),
\tag{7}
\]

for suitable rational functions \(A,B,C\) of \(a,b,c\). Since (4) says

\[
\operatorname{Im}R_1=\operatorname{Im}R_2=\operatorname{Im}R_3=0,

it follows from (7) that

\[
4\bigl(P(M)-P(N)\bigr)=0.

\[
P(M)=P(N).
